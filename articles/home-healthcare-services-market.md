# Home Healthcare Services Market: USD 641.0 Billion Forecast by 2030

The global home healthcare services market is projected to reach USD 641.0 billion by 2030, expanding at a compound annual growth rate (CAGR) of 8.93% between 2025 and 2030. Starting from USD 384.8 billion in 2024 and climbing to USD 418.0 billion by end-of-2025, this growth trajectory reflects a fundamental shift in how aging populations access care. The expansion is being driven by demographic urgency—elderly populations are doubling, chronic diseases dominate global mortality, and healthcare systems are increasingly viewing home-based solutions as both more humane and cost-effective than institutional care.

## What's Changed: AI Robots and Paradigm Shifts

The sector is moving beyond traditional in-home nursing. In 2025, companies like Cera are deploying AI-powered droid-like robots that assist elderly individuals with daily reminders, medication adherence, and continuous health monitoring—while collecting predictive health data in real time. This technological inflection is opening a new category of care entirely: remote, continuous, and increasingly autonomous.

The U.S. FDA underscored this shift in 2024 by launching the *Home as a Health Care Hub* initiative, officially repositioning the home from a care location to a vital healthcare system component. This policy signal validates the market expansion and removes regulatory friction for device integration and service delivery at home.

Major players are moving fast. CVS Health launched an AI-powered mobile app in 2025 featuring personalized health to-do lists and integrated chat. Amedisys partnered with CMS to launch the Contessa dementia care model. Air Liquide is expanding outpatient intensive care capacity in Germany through acquisitions. These aren't incremental feature updates—they're structural commitments to reshaping care delivery.

## The Demographics Are Unavoidable

People aged 65 and older make up 10.3% of the global population today. Between now and 2074, this share will double to 20.7%, with the 80+ population growing even faster. This isn't a U.S. phenomenon: Asia-Pacific will see the proportion of people aged 60+ rise from 12.2% in 2024 to 22.9% by 2050, according to WHO projections. Even as life expectancy increases, many older adults spend those additional years in poor health, requiring continuous care and assistance.

Home healthcare directly addresses this need. It's more cost-effective than hospitalization, allows patients to remain in familiar environments (improving outcomes), and frees hospital capacity for acute cases. As systems reach saturation, home care becomes not optional but essential.

## Healthcare Spending Is Breaking Records—and Flowing Toward Home

Global healthcare expenditure is accelerating faster than economic growth. The U.S. saw healthcare spending surge 7.5% in 2023 to reach USD 4.9 trillion, according to the Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services. The UK's healthcare spending climbed to approximately USD 343.11 billion in 2024. In the Netherlands, healthcare expenditure jumped 8.1% year-over-year in 2024 to USD 123 billion, rising from 9.8% to 10.0% of GDP—outpacing overall economic growth.

This is significant: when healthcare spending outpaces GDP growth, it signals systemic pressure. Home healthcare offers a release valve: it delivers quality care at lower per-episode cost while reducing hospital overhead and readmission rates. Payers and governments are recognizing this economics, and capital is flowing accordingly.

## Chronic Disease Burden Drives Urgency

Noncommunicable diseases now account for 74% of all deaths worldwide, according to the World Health Organization. Heart disease, stroke, cancer, diabetes, and chronic lung conditions are the killers—and they all require long-term management. These conditions don't resolve; they need continuous monitoring, medication adjustments, and lifestyle support. Home healthcare is the delivery mechanism for this ongoing care.

Unlike acute illness, chronic disease management doesn't require hospitalization; it requires access, continuity, and personalization. Home-based care delivers all three, while reducing infection risk, improving medication adherence, and enabling patients to maintain independence and quality of life.

## Regional Divergence: North America Leads, Asia-Pacific Accelerates

**North America** dominates the home healthcare market today, powered by comprehensive government initiatives, mature insurance infrastructure, and a rapidly aging population. The FDA's *Home as a Health Care Hub* initiative signals ongoing policy support and regulatory tailwinds for the region.

**Europe** is experiencing rising healthcare expenditure across the board. The Netherlands, UK, Germany, and France are all investing heavily in healthcare infrastructure, with spending growing faster than GDP. This creates favorable conditions for cost-efficient alternatives like home healthcare, which eases pressure on overtaxed hospital systems.

**Asia-Pacific** is the fastest-growing region. The demographic shift is acute: the 60+ population will nearly double in proportion by 2050, and many countries are still building healthcare infrastructure. Home healthcare offers a scalable, lower-cost pathway to manage this surge. Countries like China, India, and Southeast Asia are witnessing accelerating demand for accessible, affordable in-home care solutions.

**Rest of World** (Latin America, Middle East, Africa) is in earlier stages but gaining momentum. Brazil, the UAE, and South Africa are actively investing in home-based care infrastructure as they seek cost-effective ways to improve care delivery and reduce burden on traditional facilities.

## Obstacles Remain: Trust and Awareness

Despite the tailwinds, limited awareness and trust in home healthcare—especially in developing regions—remain genuine barriers. In many parts of the world, patients and families are either unaware of home-based care options or skeptical of their effectiveness. Preference for hospital-based treatment persists even when home care would be more convenient, cost-effective, and clinically appropriate. This awareness gap means the sector's full potential remains untapped in significant markets, and education and trust-building remain critical to unlocking growth.
